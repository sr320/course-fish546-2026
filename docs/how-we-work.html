How We Work

The weekly loop, where things live, the notebook entry, and the rubrics

The weekly loop

Everything in this course runs on one repeating cycle. There is one deliverable per week, it lives in one place, and it is always structured the same way.

  1. Read the week’s lecture page before Tuesday.
  2. Do the week’s analysis in your repository. In Weeks 1 to 4 this is a shared exercise; in Weeks 5 to 8 it is the week’s method applied to your own project (a canned fallback is always provided).
  3. Write notebooks/weekNN.qmd from the template (five sections, below) and render it.
  4. Push by Friday 5:00pm.
  5. Post the commit link as a comment on your Project Proposal issue. The instructor replies there with feedback.

That is the whole submission contract. Weekly entries are 60% of your grade; the mid-quarter and final presentations are 15%; the final compendium is 25%. Your best 8 of 9 weekly entries count.

Deadlines

Weekly entry: Friday 5:00 PM (Monday Nov 30 for Thanksgiving week; see the Schedule). Late work loses 40% and is not accepted more than 5 days past due. Because the best 8 of 9 entries count, one missed week does not need an excuse.

Where things live

Place What goes there How
Your repository (in the course org, created from the project template) All code, outputs, weekly notebook entries, README, final compendium Clone, commit, push. Raw data are recorded, not committed.
Course repository (issues) One Project Proposal issue per student (your home base for the quarter); Help Request issues when you are stuck Use the issue forms. Weekly commit links and tutorial completion blocks go as comments on your proposal issue.
Slack (genefish.slack.com) Quick questions, announcements, scheduling Day to day chat. Anything that needs a record goes in an issue instead.
Roberts Lab Handbook (robertslab.github.io/resources) The reference standard for computing practices, data management, Raven, and Klone Keep it open in a tab.

Weekly entry rubric

Each entry is scored out of 100.

Analysis 30 Method fits the data and question; intermediate checks are shown; results are interpreted correctly and cautiously. Method is appropriate and includes some checks; interpretation is reasonable. Method is attempted but weakly justified, unchecked, or misread. Analysis is absent, broken, or inappropriate.
Evidence of work 20 Links to commits, reports, figures, tables, or logs give concrete proof of progress. Evidence is present and generally supports the claims. Evidence is thin, hard to inspect, or only partially connected. Evidence is missing or cannot be accessed.
Reproducible practice 15 Layout, paths, commit history, and documentation make the work easy to rerun and inspect. Most materials are organized and version-controlled. Organization is inconsistent, paths unclear, or outputs stale. Work cannot be followed from the repository.
Reflection questions 15 Answers show reasoning, connect to the week’s material, and relate to the student’s own project. Answers are correct and complete. Answers are partial or superficial. Answers are missing.
Blockers and help-seeking 10 Blockers are precise, with attempted fixes and a linked Help Request when needed. Blockers or risks are named with enough context to respond. Blockers are vague or raised late. Blockers are absent despite stalled progress.
Next-week plan 10 Next steps are concrete, prioritized, and sized for one week. Next steps are stated and plausible. Next steps are too broad or not actionable. Next steps are missing.

Final compendium

The final compendium is the complete project package in your repository at the Week 10 tag. It should include a top-level README.md, final rendered report, source .qmd or scripts, key outputs, a data availability statement, a software and platform statement, and a link to your Project Proposal issue.

Criterion Points Excellent Complete Developing Incomplete
Research question and context 10 Question is clear, biologically meaningful, and grounded in appropriate background. Question and context are understandable. Question is present but broad, shifting, or weakly motivated. Question or context is missing.
Data provenance and organization 15 Data sources, sample structure, file locations, raw/processed distinction, and data availability are explicit. Main data sources and project structure are described. Some data details are unclear or scattered. Data source, structure, or availability cannot be determined.
Workflow and reproducibility 20 A reader can trace the workflow from input to output; code, commands, environment notes, and render instructions are sufficient to rerun or audit the analysis. Workflow is mostly reproducible with minor gaps. Workflow can be partially followed but requires guessing. Workflow is not reproducible from the repository.
Technical execution 15 Methods are appropriate for the data type and project question; QC and intermediate checks support the analysis. Methods are generally appropriate and include some checks. Methods are attempted but weakly justified or missing important checks. Analysis is technically absent, broken, or inappropriate.
Results and evidence 15 Figures, tables, and summaries directly support the main result and are clearly labeled. Results are present and mostly connected to the question. Results are thin, hard to interpret, or disconnected from the question. Results are missing or not inspectable.
Interpretation and biological reasoning 10 Claims are proportional to the evidence and distinguish results from interpretation. Interpretation is reasonable but may be limited. Interpretation overreaches, stays superficial, or misses key uncertainty. Biological interpretation is missing.
Caveats and next steps 10 Limitations are specific, honest, and connected to concrete next analyses or data needs. Main caveats and next steps are included. Caveats are generic or next steps are vague. Caveats or next steps are missing.
Communication and polish 5 Report and README are readable, well structured, linked, and free of avoidable confusion. Materials are readable with minor rough spots. Materials are difficult to scan or inconsistently formatted. Materials are too incomplete or confusing to evaluate.

Baseline expectations

  • Do not commit large raw data files unless the instructor has explicitly approved it.
  • Link to exact commits, not only the repository homepage.
  • Keep source files and rendered outputs together or clearly linked.
  • Label figures and tables so they can be understood outside the live presentation.
  • Document software, platforms, and major package versions when they affect reproducibility.
  • Explain what did not work. Failed or partial analyses earn credit when they are documented clearly and used to motivate next steps.

Presentations

The presentation grade covers the Week 5 mid-quarter update and the Week 10 final talk. The goal is to explain the project clearly enough that another student can understand the question, workflow, strongest evidence, and remaining uncertainty.

Criterion Points Excellent Complete Developing Incomplete
Project framing 15 Research question, motivation, and data type are immediately clear. Question and data type are understandable. Framing is present but hard to follow. Framing is missing.
Workflow explanation 20 Workflow is explained at the right level, with key inputs, tools, decisions, and checks. Workflow is mostly clear. Workflow is too vague, too detailed, or missing important steps. Workflow cannot be understood.
Evidence and results 25 Figures or tables are legible, relevant, and support the main claim. Results are shown and generally interpreted. Results are present but hard to read or weakly connected to the claim. Results are missing.
Interpretation and limits 20 Speaker clearly separates what the evidence supports from what remains uncertain. Interpretation and at least one limitation are included. Interpretation is superficial or caveats are generic. Interpretation or limitations are missing.
Delivery and discussion 20 Talk is organized, fits the time, uses clear visuals, and handles questions thoughtfully. Talk is understandable and mostly within time. Talk is hard to follow, too rushed, or visually cluttered. Talk is not prepared or cannot be evaluated.

Grade interpretation

Range Meaning
3.7-4.0 Strong, reproducible, clearly interpreted work
3.0-3.6 Solid work with minor gaps in reproducibility, evidence, or explanation
2.0-2.9 Meaningful progress, but important pieces are unclear, incomplete, or weakly connected
1.0-1.9 Limited evidence or substantial reproducibility and interpretation gaps
Below 1.0 Missing, inaccessible, or too incomplete to evaluate reliably

Skills reference

Short reminders for the tools you will use every week. The tutorials on the Setup page and the Roberts Lab Handbook are the full references.

RStudio and Raven

Raven is home for most weekly work. You should be able to log in to RStudio Server, pull your repository, open the project from the repository root, run R or shell code from a Quarto document, save figures and tables to output/, and commit and push. Raven may require the UW VPN off campus.

Quarto

Quarto is the format for notebook entries and the final report because it keeps code, results, and explanation together.

  • Use relative paths from the repository root.
  • Render from a clean session before pushing.
  • Include enough narrative that another student can follow your reasoning.
  • Save important outputs as files, not only as printed tables in HTML.
  • Generate figures from code whenever possible.

GitHub Issues

Two forms, on the course repository:

  • Project Proposal. One per student, opened in Week 0 or 1. Your weekly commit links and tutorial completion blocks go here as comments. Edit the top post as the project evolves.
  • Help Request. When you are stuck. Include the platform, the exact command, the exact error text, what you already tried, and a link to the relevant commit or output.

Hyak and containers

Use Hyak when a step is too large, slow, or environment-sensitive for Raven. For every job, record in your notebook entry the SLURM script, job ID, container path or environment, input and output paths, and the relevant lines of slurm-*.out. Never run heavy analyses on the login node.

Archiving and the compendium

Archiving is a habit, not a final step. By Week 10 your repository should have a clear top-level README.md, the scripts or Quarto files that generate the final outputs, key output tables and figures, a data availability note, software and platform notes, and a link to your Project Proposal issue. If raw data cannot be committed, document where they came from, how they were named, and how checksums or manifests were recorded.

When something breaks

  1. Confirm which platform you are on.
  2. Confirm the working directory.
  3. Confirm the input file exists.
  4. Copy the exact command and error.
  5. Decide whether the problem is path, permissions, software, memory or time, or data format.
  6. Open a Help Request if you are still stuck.
